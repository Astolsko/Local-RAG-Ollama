# Meridian Harbor Authority — Annual Risk Report 2024

## Purpose

This report is prepared annually by the Authority's risk committee and presented to the Board
of Commissioners. It records the principal risks to the Authority's operations, finances, and
statutory obligations, together with the mitigations in place and the residual risk rating
assigned by the committee.

Ratings are assigned on a five-point scale from Low to Severe. A rating of High or above
requires a named executive owner and quarterly reporting to the Board.

## 1. Climate and Extreme Weather

**Residual rating: High.**

Meridian Bay has recorded a mean sea level rise of 19 centimeters since the Authority's
founding in 1974. Storm surge modeling commissioned in 2023 indicates that the quay elevation
at Alder Terminal, the oldest of the four terminals, will be overtopped by a one-in-fifty-year
surge event before 2040 without intervention.

Berth closures attributable to weather totaled 61 hours in 2024, up from 44 hours in 2023 and
38 hours in 2022. The committee notes this is the third consecutive annual increase.

Mitigations include a quay-raising study for Alder Terminal due to report in 2025, revised
mooring standards issued in March 2024, and a surge barrier feasibility assessment carried
out jointly with the national waterways agency.

## 2. Capital Program Delivery

**Residual rating: High.**

The Kestrel Terminal program represents the largest concentration of capital risk the
Authority has ever carried. Schedule slippage or cost overrun on Kestrel would materially
affect the Authority's borrowing capacity and its ability to fund the climate adaptation work
described in section 1.

The committee draws particular attention to the dependency between civil works completion and
automation equipment installation. Delay in either workstream propagates to the commissioning
date. The committee notes that the automation supplier's published delivery record attributes
most historical delay to customer-side civil works, and recommends that the Authority not
assume its own civil program is immune to the same pattern.

Mitigations include monthly integrated schedule review, liquidated damages provisions in the
automation contract, and a contingency reserve held at 9 percent of approved program budget.

## 3. Cybersecurity

**Residual rating: High.**

Terminal automation materially increases the Authority's exposure to cyber incidents. A
successful intrusion into a terminal operating system could halt cargo movement across an
entire terminal, and in the automated configuration planned for Kestrel there is no practical
manual fallback for sustained operation.

The Authority recorded 14 reportable security incidents in 2024, none of which resulted in
operational disruption. Twelve were phishing attempts against administrative staff; two were
unauthorized access attempts against externally facing systems.

The committee flags that software update provisions in the automation specification permit
supplier-initiated changes within agreed maintenance windows, and recommends that rollback
capability and update authorization be verified independently before provisional acceptance.

Mitigations include network segmentation between administrative and operational technology
environments, a 24-hour security operations contract, and annual penetration testing.

## 4. Labor Relations

**Residual rating: Severe.**

The collective agreement covering dockworkers at all four terminals expires on
31 December 2026, approximately three quarters before Kestrel Terminal is scheduled to
commission. The committee assesses the coincidence of these dates as the single most
significant near-term operational risk facing the Authority.

Automation-driven headcount reduction is the principal unresolved issue. The Authority has
declined to offer contractual headcount floors for crane operators. A work stoppage during
the Kestrel commissioning window would affect both the new terminal's proving period and
throughput at the three operating terminals simultaneously.

Mitigations include an established retraining fund, early engagement with the recognized labor
representative on training content for the new equipment, and contingency planning for
partial operations.

## 5. Concentration of Supplier Exposure

**Residual rating: Medium.**

All automated cargo handling equipment at Kestrel Terminal, together with the software
integration layer that connects it to the terminal operating system, is sourced from a single
supplier. The committee notes that this concentration is a deliberate consequence of seeking
integrated system responsibility, but records that it leaves the Authority without a
straightforward alternative in the event of supplier financial distress or sustained
performance failure.

Mitigations include supplier financial monitoring, source-code escrow for the software
integration layer, and spare-parts stockholding requirements written into the contract.

## 6. Revenue Concentration

**Residual rating: Medium.**

The Authority's three largest customers accounted for 47 percent of cargo tariff revenue in
2024. The largest single customer accounted for 21 percent. Loss of any one of the three to a
competing regional port would require either tariff adjustment across remaining customers or
deferral of discretionary capital spending.

## Committee Observations

The committee draws the Board's attention to the degree of interdependence among the risks
above. Climate adaptation is funded from capacity that Kestrel cost overruns would consume;
Kestrel's commissioning is exposed to a labor agreement expiring months beforehand; and the
automation that drives the labor risk is also the source of the cybersecurity and supplier
concentration risks. The committee recommends that the Board consider these six risks as a
single connected system rather than as independent line items.
