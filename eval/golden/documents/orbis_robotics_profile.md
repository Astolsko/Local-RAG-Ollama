# Orbis Robotics — Company Profile

## History

Orbis Robotics N.V. was founded in Rotterdam in 1996 by Willem Draaisma and Sofia Marchetti,
initially as a controls-engineering consultancy serving inland barge terminals. The company
entered the crane automation market in 2004 with the release of its first automated stacking
crane control stack, and delivered its first fully automated container yard at the Port of
Algeciras in 2008.

The company relocated its headquarters from Rotterdam to Antwerp in 2015 to consolidate
engineering and manufacturing on a single site. It retains a controls software office in
Rotterdam employing roughly 90 engineers.

Willem Draaisma served as chief executive from founding until 2018. Sofia Marchetti, who had
led the company's software division, succeeded him and remains chief executive today.
Draaisma continues to serve as a non-executive member of the supervisory board.

## Scale and Financials

Orbis Robotics employs approximately 2,400 people across six countries. Reported revenue for
the 2023 financial year was 1.12 billion euros, of which 63 percent came from new equipment
sales and 37 percent from service, spare parts, and software licensing.

The company has been majority-owned since 2019 by Havenstroom Holding, a Netherlands-based
industrial investment group, which holds 58 percent of issued shares. The remainder is
publicly traded on Euronext Brussels.

## Product Lines

### Ship-to-shore gantry cranes

The **Meridian-class STS** is the company's current flagship ship-to-shore crane, introduced
in 2022. It is offered in outreach configurations from 58 to 74 meters and rated lifting
capacities from 50 to 80 tonnes under spreader. The Meridian-class was the first Orbis crane
to ship with regenerative hoist braking as standard rather than as an option.

### Automated stacking cranes

The **ASC-9** is the ninth generation of the company's automated stacking crane, released in
2021. It supports stacking heights up to 1 over 6 containers and rail gauges between 26 and
34 meters. Orbis quotes a positioning tolerance of plus or minus 15 millimeters for the ASC-9
across its full rail gauge range.

### Automated guided vehicles

Orbis supplies battery-electric automated guided vehicles under the **Drift** product name.
Since 2020 all Drift vehicles have shipped with lithium iron phosphate cells, replacing the
lithium nickel manganese cobalt chemistry used in earlier generations. The change was made
after a 2019 thermal incident at a customer site in Valencia, described in the company's
2019 annual report as the trigger for a full chemistry review.

### Software

The **Orbis Yard Director** terminal operating system interface is licensed separately from
equipment. It is the integration layer through which Orbis cranes and vehicles exchange
instructions with a customer's terminal operating system.

## Notable Contracts

- **2008 — Port of Algeciras.** First fully automated container yard delivered by the company.
- **2016 — Port of Tanjung Pelepas.** 28 automated stacking cranes; the company's largest
  ASC order at the time.
- **2021 — Port of Gothenburg.** Retrofit of existing stacking cranes to ASC-9 controls.
- **2024 — Kestrel Terminal, Meridian Bay.** Supply and integration of 12 Meridian-class
  ship-to-shore cranes, 44 ASC-9 automated stacking cranes, and 96 Drift automated guided
  vehicles. This is the largest single contract in the company's history by value.

## Delivery Record

Orbis has publicly reported on-time provisional acceptance for 81 percent of automation
projects delivered since 2015. The company attributes the majority of its delayed deliveries
to civil works readiness on the customer side rather than equipment supply, a position
disputed by two customers in published arbitration summaries.

The company carries a backlog of 1.9 billion euros as of the end of the 2023 financial year,
which it reports as approximately 21 months of equipment revenue at current delivery rates.
