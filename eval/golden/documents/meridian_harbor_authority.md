# Meridian Harbor Authority — Organizational Overview

## Founding and Mandate

The Meridian Harbor Authority (MHA) was established in 1974 by the Coastal Governance Act
as an independent public corporation. Its mandate is to operate, maintain, and expand the
deep-water port facilities at Meridian Bay, and to regulate commercial navigation within
the harbor approaches out to twelve nautical miles.

The Authority is self-funding. It receives no direct appropriation and covers its operating
costs through berthing fees, cargo tariffs, and long-term lease income from terminal
operators. In the 2023 fiscal year the Authority reported total operating revenue of
418 million dollars against operating expenses of 361 million dollars.

## Governance

The Authority is directed by a nine-member Board of Commissioners. Six commissioners are
appointed by the regional governor to staggered six-year terms; the remaining three are
elected by the member municipalities of the Meridian Bay Compact.

Since March 2021 the Board has been chaired by Commissioner Adaeze Okonkwo, a former
maritime logistics executive. The Authority's day-to-day operations are led by Executive
Director Tomas Lindqvist, who was appointed in September 2019 after fourteen years at the
Port of Gothenburg. Executive Director Lindqvist reports directly to the Board and holds
signing authority for contracts valued under 25 million dollars; anything above that
threshold requires a recorded majority vote of the Board.

## Facilities

The harbor comprises four operating terminals:

- **Alder Terminal** — bulk commodities, primarily grain and aggregate. Two berths,
  maximum draft 11.5 meters. Commissioned 1978.
- **Birchwood Terminal** — refrigerated and general cargo. Three berths, maximum draft
  12.0 meters. Commissioned 1986, refurbished 2011.
- **Cormorant Terminal** — roll-on/roll-off vehicle traffic. Two berths, maximum draft
  9.8 meters. Commissioned 1994.
- **Kestrel Terminal** — containerized cargo. Currently under construction; see the
  Kestrel Terminal automation specification for engineering detail.

Total quay length across the operating terminals is 3,180 meters. The harbor's federal
navigation channel is maintained at a depth of 14.2 meters by a dredging program funded
jointly with the national waterways agency.

## The Kestrel Terminal Program

Kestrel Terminal is the largest capital project in the Authority's history, with an approved
budget of 890 million dollars and a scheduled commissioning date of Q3 2027. When complete
it will add four deep-water container berths with a combined annual throughput capacity of
1.8 million TEU.

The Board approved the Kestrel automation contract in a 7–2 vote on 14 June 2024. The
contract was awarded to Orbis Robotics for the supply and integration of the terminal's
automated stacking cranes and ship-to-shore gantry systems. Because the contract value
exceeded the Executive Director's signing threshold, the award required the full Board vote
described above.

Commissioner Okonkwo recused herself from the Orbis Robotics procurement evaluation, citing
a prior advisory relationship with one of the losing bidders. The recusal is recorded in the
minutes of the 14 June 2024 session.

## Workforce

The Authority directly employs 1,240 people. An additional 3,100 workers are employed by
terminal operators and stevedoring contractors working under Authority leases. Dockworkers
at all four terminals are represented by Local 47 of the Maritime Trades Federation, whose
current collective agreement expires on 31 December 2026.

Automation of the Kestrel Terminal has been the principal point of contention in early
negotiations toward a successor agreement. Local 47 has sought contractual guarantees on
crane-operator headcount; the Authority has to date declined to offer headcount floors,
offering instead a retraining fund and attrition-based transition commitments.
